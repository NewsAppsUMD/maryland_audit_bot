School Audits
(cid:190) Audits are performed in accordance with State
law, which requires OLA to audit all 24 public
school systems once over a six-year period. As
of January 2009, 14 reports have been issued.
(cid:190) The purpose of the audits is to evaluate the
effectiveness and efficiency of the school
system’s financial management practices in
safeguarding assets and its use of financial
resources.
(cid:190) Audit approach consists of 11 functional areas
previously approved by the Joint Audit
Committee, as required by State law.
(cid:190) When warranted, we used the work of other
auditors to avoid duplication of effort.

Overview
(cid:190) FY 07 operating budget of $2.2 billion, including
payroll of $1.3 billion.
(cid:190) Enrollment of approximately 137,700 students in
199 schools.
(cid:190) Report contained 23 findings and 19
recommendations in the 11 areas reviewed.
(cid:190) Internal control problems, such as controls over
certain revenues and payroll systems, were noted
in several areas.
(cid:190) Certain operational improvements should be made
to improve effectiveness and efficiency.
(cid:190) Certain best practices were found to be in place in
several areas such as human resources, facilities,
and food services.

Key Findings – Internal Controls
Collections and Accounts Receivable – (pgs.11, 12,
48)
(cid:131) Written cash handling procedures were not
developed for collections received by remote
locations (not the finance office). FY 08 collections
by these locations totaled $8.8 million.
Consequently, control weaknesses existed.
(cid:131) Certain cash handling and accounts receivable
duties were not separated. A formal debt
collection policy was not established.
Purchasing Card Programs – (p.21)
(cid:131) Duties of requesting and receiving cards were not
separated.
(cid:131) Transactions were often not recorded on logs and
logs were often not reviewed by cardholder
supervisors.

Key Findings – Internal Controls
Payroll – (p.27)
(cid:131) A number of employees had unnecessary
modification access to the automated
personnel/payroll system and no one reviewed
reports of changes made on the payroll
system.
Information Technology – (p.34)
(cid:131) Password security was not adequate and
certain other security actions (such as
updating security software) were not always
performed.
Other Financial Controls – (p.62)
(cid:131) The eligibility of health care program
participants and their dependents were not
verified and claims were not audited.

Key Findings – Other Issues
Procurement Card Use– (p.22)
(cid:131) Policies did not address the permissibility of certain
categories of expenses. Cards were used for items
that did not appear to meet the objectives of the
school system and documentation was not retained
to ensure the propriety of all such transactions.
Materials and Supplies – (p.30)
(cid:131) MCPS did not utilize just-in-time ordering for
supplies (such as office supplies) to reduce storage
costs and better manage cash outflow.
Facilities – (p.43)
(cid:131) Certain capabilities of the work order system were
not fully used. Work orders were not completed
timely and performance measures not used to
ensure efficient operations and productivity of staff.
(cid:131) Preventive maintenance was not adequately
documented.

Key Findings – Other Issues
Transportation – (p.47)
(cid:131) Bus routing policies did not address certain
factors (such as bus capacity limits) and did not
ensure efficient operations because students
were not required to ride a specific bus route.
Available documentation showed a number of
buses either over or under-utilized.
Board Operations – (pgs.57-58)
(cid:131) The scope of work performed by the internal
auditor was limited.
(cid:131) A fraud hotline was not established.
